# Spaceport visual reference

The launch view is a side-on, schematic scene. It uses documented terrain and major pad structures, not a satellite image or surveyed building placement. Its horizontal axis follows the rocket's 2-D flight plane, so left/right positions in the artwork are composition choices.

| Scene | Evidence used | Design cues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LC-39A, Kennedy | [NASA LC-39A historic record](https://netspublic.grc.nasa.gov/main/finalMultiuseEA.pdf) | Raised hardstand, crawler ramp, flame trench, water system, flat Atlantic coastal marsh. |
| SLC-4E, Vandenberg | [US Space Force environmental map](https://www.vandenberg.spaceforce.mil/Portals/18/documents/Environmental/SpaceX_Falcon%209%20Cadence%20Increase%20Draft%20SEA%20for%20Public%20Review_COMBINED_REDUCED1.pdf?ver=VG1a6SRhBj1ahJMP9d8qQA%3D%3D) | Dry coastal terrain and inland hills, compact low pad buildings, transporter erector. |
| ELA-2, Kourou | [ESA ELA-2 image](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/1998/01/ELA-1_2_launch_pads_at_Kourou2), [ESA spaceport overview](https://www.esa.int/establishment/about-us/directorates/space-transportation/europe-s-spaceport/europe-s-spaceport) | Forest clearing, mobile service tower, rail connection to assembly building. |
| Satish Dhawan, Sriharikota | [ISRO launch services](https://www.isro.gov.in/launchservices.html) | Coastal island, mobile service tower on rails, vehicle assembly and propellant facilities. The dataset names the space centre rather than a particular pad; the scene draws cues from the First Launch Pad. |
| Site 1/5, Baikonur | [ESA Soyuz rollout](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2019/07/Soyuz_rollout_for_Beyond_mission2), [ESA support arms](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2009/06/Soyuz_launch_pad_primary_support_arms) | Dry steppe, railway, four green support arms represented as opposed pairs in side view. |

The rocket model is selected independently of the pad. A vehicle from the game roster can appear at a site that has not hosted that vehicle; the art represents the selected site, not a documented historical launch configuration.
